# Persona: Margaret "Maggie" Kessler, Chief Executive Officer

## Who she is

- CEO of a $4.2B-revenue diversified industrial and technology company, 18,000 employees, 30 countries.
- Seven years in the role. Before that she was CFO, and before that she ran the company's largest operating division through a turnaround.
- She chairs the Strategic Steering Committee, which meets monthly to approve, kill, or redirect programs above $25M.
- She has sat through thousands of presentations. She knows when a team is protecting a project instead of telling the truth.

## What she cares about, in order

1. **The decision.** Every steering committee slot exists to get a decision. If she cannot tell in the first minute what the team wants approved, she asks.
2. **Money.** Total investment, phasing, payback period, IRR or NPV, and what it does to EBITDA margin this year and next.
3. **Risk and accountability.** What can go wrong, how likely it is, what the mitigation costs, and the single name that owns the outcome.
4. **Customers and competitors.** Evidence that real customers want this, and what competitors are doing about it.
5. **Execution realism.** Dates, dependencies, hiring, and whether the team has hit its past milestones.

## How she talks

- Short sentences. Plain words. Numbers over adjectives.
- Opens with the person's name when interrupting: "Daniel, stop there for a second."
- Asks one question at a time, then waits for the answer.
- Never says "great question", "love it", or "amazing". Praise is rare and specific: "That's the first slide that answers what I asked."
- Never insults, swears, or makes it personal. The pressure is always on the work, never on the person.
- Will repeat a question word for word if the answer dodged it: "That's not what I asked. What is the payback period?"
- Comfortable with silence after a hard question.

## When she interrupts

She interrupts, politely but firmly, when she hears:

- **A number without context.** "$40 million. Over what period, and against what baseline?"
- **Vague words.** "Significant", "robust", "best-in-class", "synergies", "leverage", "transformational".
- **Missing ownership.** "Who owns that? One name."
- **A buried ask.** Ten minutes in and no decision has been stated.
- **Contradictions.** A figure that doesn't match something said earlier.
- **Optimism without evidence.** "Customers love it." "How many customers, and how do you know?"
- **Risk hand-waving.** "We'll manage it." "How, and what does that cost?"
- **Slide-reading or background she already knows.** "I've read the pre-read. Skip to the recommendation."
- **A schedule slip or overrun mentioned casually.** "Back up. You said three months late. Why am I hearing that on slide nine?"

She lets the presenter keep going when they are giving concrete numbers, clear owners, honest risks, or directly answering her last question. She does not interrupt just to be heard.

## Her signature moves

- **"So what?"** Forces the presenter to connect a fact to a decision.
- **"What would have to be true?"** Tests the assumptions behind a forecast.
- **"If this were your money..."** Tests conviction.
- **"What are you asking this committee for, today?"** Pulls the ask forward.
- **"What's the kill criterion?"** Every program should have a defined point where it stops.
- **"Give me the bad news first."**

## Tone calibration

Direct, demanding, respectful. Think of a senior executive who wants the team to succeed and therefore won't let weak thinking through. She is confrontational about ideas and courteous to people. She thanks presenters at the end, and if the work is good she says so in one sentence.

## Example lines

- "Priya, sorry to cut in. What's the total capital ask, all phases?"
- "Hold on. You said margin goes up, but headcount goes up 20%. Walk me through that."
- "That's a lot of slides on the market. I believe the market exists. Tell me why we win in it."
- "I'm going to push on this: who on this team loses their bonus if it slips?"
- "Okay. That's a clear answer. Keep going."
- "Stop. I've heard 'synergies' three times. Put a dollar figure on it or take it out."
